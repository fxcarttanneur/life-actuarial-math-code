# Practical Introduction to Life Actuarial Mathematics

### Interest, Mortality, Annuities, Insurance and Reserves — with Python

**Companion repository for the book**

[**Get the book on Amazon**](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0HRKQ1HJD)

This repository provides the Python code, Jupyter notebooks and accompanying resources for *Practical Introduction to Life Actuarial Mathematics* by F.-X. Cart-Tanneur.

The purpose of the book and its companion resources is to connect the mathematical foundations of life actuarial science with practical applications. Each topic is introduced intuitively, developed mathematically and illustrated through worked examples that can be reproduced, explored and modified using Python.

The repository is designed to support independent study, university coursework and practical exploration of life actuarial mathematics.

---

## What the book covers

The book develops the principal mathematical concepts and techniques used in life insurance and actuarial calculations.

Topics include:

- **Interest theory:** compound interest, annuities, present values and accumulated values.
- **Mortality mathematics:** life tables, survival probabilities, the force of mortality and Gompertz–Makeham laws.
- **Life insurance:** whole life and endowment insurance, with actuarial commutation functions.
- **Annuities and pensions:** pure endowments, term annuities, joint-life and survivor pensions.
- **Premium calculations:** net premiums and commercial tariffs incorporating acquisition, management and collection expenses.
- **Actuarial reserves:** prospective and retrospective reserves, savings and risk premiums, and Zillmerised reserves.
- **Policy applications:** surrenders, paid-up policies, policy loans and borrower's insurance.

## Python and Jupyter resources

The accompanying code is an integral part of the learning approach.

It allows readers to:

- Reproduce the numerical examples developed in the book.
- Explore actuarial calculations through Python.
- Experiment with different interest and mortality assumptions.
- Modify parameters and investigate their impact on premiums and reserves.
- Develop a practical understanding of the mathematical relationships underlying life insurance products.

The resources are intended to complement the theoretical explanations, not replace them.

## Who is this resource for?

This book and repository are intended for:

- **University students** studying actuarial science, mathematics, finance or insurance.
- **Lecturers and academics** looking for practical examples to complement actuarial mathematics courses.
- **Self-learners** developing their understanding of life actuarial mathematics.
- **Actuarial examination candidates** wishing to reinforce theoretical concepts through numerical applications.
- **Junior actuaries and insurance professionals** seeking a clearer understanding of the calculations underlying life insurance products.

A background in mathematics and basic familiarity with Python will be helpful.

## How to use this repository

The repository is intended to be used alongside the book.

1. Browse the available Python code, Jupyter notebooks and datasets.
2. Download or clone the repository.
3. Open the notebooks in a Jupyter environment.
4. Run the examples and explore the calculations.
5. Modify the assumptions and parameters to investigate alternative scenarios.

The code is provided so that readers can reproduce the examples and develop their own understanding through experimentation.
